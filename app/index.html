    The Guide How to use Music Scribe
    Quick start
    1. Tune first (under the Begin seal). Play one string at a time until each gets a ✓.
    2. Tick Metronome if you want a steady tempo (a one-bar count-in, then a tick on every beat).
    3. Tap Begin and play. A rough preview appears as you go.
    4. Tap Cease. Mozart has been writing while you played, so the real page is ready in a few seconds.
    5. Tap any note to hear it or fix it. ▶ plays it all back. Export makes a PDF, image, MusicXML, MIDI…
    Set up your room for recording
    • Quiet first. Turn off fans, air conditioning, the TV and anything that hums. Close windows and doors.
    • Soft rooms sound best. Rugs, curtains, a sofa or a bed soak up echo. Avoid bathrooms, kitchens and bare corners.
    • Mic placement. Put the phone or laptop 30–60 cm (1–2 ft) away, pointing at where the neck meets the body (around the 12th–14th fret). Right at the soundhole sounds boomy.
    • Keep it still. Rest the device on a table or stand, not in your hand or on your lap, and don't touch it while you play. Use the same spot every time.
    • Check the level. Watch the meter by Sensitivity: a firm strum should reach the middle, not the very end. Move closer or adjust Sensitivity if it doesn't.
    • Headphones for the click and layers. Wired ones are best: Bluetooth adds a delay. If you use speakers, keep them low and pointed away from the mic.
    • Play cleanly. Let notes ring, mute strings you're not playing, and lift fingers gently (Mozart filters finger-lift noise, but less is better). Fresh strings help.
    • On a phone, turn on Do Not Disturb (alerts get written down as notes too) and plug in for long sessions.
    Fixing and shaping the manuscript
    • Tap a note to hear it and open the editor. Every change plays the new note.
    • ▲ ▼, W S, + − or ↑ ↓: a step up or down in the key. ♭ ♯ or Shift: one fret. ±8ve: an octave.
    • A D or ← →: previous / next note. Delete: remove it (the rest slides up). Space: play / pause. Ctrl+Z: undo.
    • + Bar, Duplicate bar and Delete bar (under the manuscript) work on the bar you've tapped a note or rest in, in every layer. Pick notes in several bars to duplicate or delete just those bars. With nothing tapped, + Bar adds one at the end.
    • On a PC: Ctrl+click adds a note to the selection, Shift+click selects everything in between, Ctrl+Shift+click picks whole bars. Then Ctrl+C copy, Ctrl+X cut, Ctrl+V paste, Ctrl+D delete, Ctrl+A select all, Esc let go. Notes paste into the rest you tap, or straight after the note you tap; bars paste as new bars.
    • Chords: tap a note or rest and choose from Add chord to bar …. You hear it, and it fills that bar in the layer you're editing, with its name and a playable shape. Choose another to swap it. Chords that fit your key come first.
    • Tap a rest to add a note there, Remove rest (the notes after it slide up), or Hold the note before through it.
    • + New take (in Thy Library) keeps the take you have and starts another: Record a take opens a clean page for Begin; Write one gives you four empty bars to fill.
    • Add a saved take as a layer: in Thy Library, press the stacked-sheets button with a + beside a take. It's copied onto your manuscript as a new layer (up to 4); the saved take stays as it is.
    • Add a note into a rest: the first one fills it; after that, each new note takes the length you used last.
    • Odd time signatures: in Time, choose Type my own… and type any top number, or groups such as 3+3+2, over 2, 4, 8 or 16. Groups set the beaming and the metronome's accents. Yours are kept in the list.
    • Select (under the manuscript) picks several notes at once to move or remove together. While it is on, the page has a dashed gold border; Esc turns it off.
    • Detail: Simple for a clean, readable page; Detailed keeps every rest and 16th note.
    • Practice: slow it down, loop it, add a metronome to the playback.
    • 30 sounds: choose one in Sounds like, or press 🔎 to search, hear each one and star your favorites (they go to the top).
    • Drum beats: choose one beside Metronome. It plays while you record (after a one-bar count-in), in playback, loops and Jam, at your tempo and time signature. Fills and Drums volume are beside it. While recording, the drums are thinned out so speakers don't add wrong notes; wearing headphones? Tick Headphones for the full sound.
    • Choose what loops: tap a bar's number to select it; tap again to unselect. The loop runs from the first selected bar to the last. With none selected (or All), the whole manuscript loops. Jam uses the same bars.
    • Fixes counts what you corrected. Save that log and share it to help Mozart improve.
    Layers: building a quartet
    • Record your melody first, ideally with the metronome on, so the tempo is steady. Tempo and time are shared by every layer, and locked once there are two.
    • + Layer adds a part. Tap Begin: one bar of count-in, then the other layers play while you play your backup part. Wear headphones.
    • The layer you're editing is bold black; the others are faint: sky blue, violet, arterial red, evergreen. All shows every layer bold; tap one to make it thin.
    • 🔊/🔇 mutes a layer; Volume balances them. Each layer sits a little left or right so you can tell them apart.
    • Sounds like picks the instrument a layer plays back with: ten sounds, a different one for each layer if you like. MIDI and MusicXML exports carry it too.
    • Jam along with this loops the bars you select (or the whole manuscript) while you play freely. Tap Record and your part is written from the next time the loop comes round; then listen to each time round and Keep the best as a layer.
    • + 3rd / + 6th copies the layer you're on a 3rd or a 6th higher, in the key: an instant harmony to try against the melody.
    • Ideas: a bass line on the low strings (root notes on beat 1), chords strummed on the beat, or a simple counter-melody in the gaps.
    • Export → MusicXML (All layers) opens in MuseScore or Sibelius as a real multi-part score; MIDI gives a track per layer.
    Tuning and capo
    • Tune before every session, and again if you change strings or the room warms up. Out-of-tune strings get written as the wrong notes.
    • Tap a string in the tuner to tune just that one (handy in a noisy room).
    • Playing in Drop D, half-step down or another tuning? Choose it under Tuning before you record, so the tab shows the right frets. Custom sets each string.
    • Capo: set the fret and the tab is counted from the capo (open C shape with capo 2 reads 0-1-0-2-3), while the notation shows the real sounding notes. Tune with the capo off.
